MORE THAN A BUILDING
There are certain moments in a community ’s history that remind us we
are not simply maintaining a town—we are shaping its future. The opening
of Manchester’s new downtown library is one of those moments.
As Editor of Manchester Now, I’ve had the privilege of sharing many of the
exciting changes taking place throughout our community over the past
several years. I’ve also heard the conversations that naturally accompany
public investment. People have different opinions about priorities, costs,
and timing, and those conversations are an important p art of a healthy
community. But beyond those discussions is a more important question:
What kind of community are we building for the next generation?
Our new library is about so much more than books. It is a place where
children will discover a love of le arning, students will find oppor tunities to
succeed, entrepreneurs will access resources, seniors will connect, and
neighbors will come together. It will also ser ve as an impor tant anchor for
our downtown, bringing increase d activity that supports local businesses,
restaurants, and economic growth. Strong public sp aces help create
strong local economies.
That same philosophy has guided Manchester’s investments over the
past decade. Since 2012, our community has made a commitment to
reinvesting in the places that shape everyday life. We’ve transforme d our
schools into modern le arning environments, cre ated The Oak and East
Side Plaza, invested in p arks and trails, and are planning for a new senior
center based on the evolving needs of our residents.
These are not simply buildings or projects. They are investments in
education, recreation, culture, health, economic vitality, and quality of life.
They create spaces where people connect, where memories are made,
and where families, businesses, and future generations can continue to
thrive.
There is certainly a cost to investing in the future. But there is also a cost
to standing still. Aging infrastructure does not improve with time, and
delaying important investments often cre ates greater challenges and
expenses in the future. Previous generations made decisions that shaped
the Manchester we know today. It is now our responsibilit y to continue
that legacy.
With that, I encourage you to explore this issue of Manchester Now
and discover the many recreation, cultural, educational, and leisure
opportunities waiting for you this fall. Visit our parks and trails, exp erience
our new downtown library, enjoy community events, and spend time in the
public spaces that continue to bring Manchester to life. These places were
created not simply to be admired, but to be enjoyed, shared, and filled
with memories. We look forward to seeing you out in the community this
season, exp eriencing firsthand the places and spaces that are helping
Manchester thrive—today and for generations to come.

LEVEL 1
The child must b e at least 5 years old and able to be in the water
without a parent .
LEVEL 2
The child must be able to enter/exit water using the ramp or
ladder, front and back float independently for 5 seconds, front
and back glide for two b ody lengths, roll from front to back and roll
from back to front.
LEVEL 3
The child must b e able to fully submerge and hold their breath for
10 seconds, recover to a vertical position from front/b ack float or
glide and back float for 15 seconds.

LEVEL 4
The child must b e able to enter the water by jumping from side of
pool (de ep end) fully submerge and recover to surface, front crawl
for 15 yards, b ackstroke for 15 yards and tread water for 1 minute.
LEVEL 5
The child must b e able to front crawl for 25 yards, backstroke for
15 yards, butterfly for 15 yards and swim underwater for 3-5 body
lengths
LEVEL 6
The child must b e able to front crawl for 50 yards, breaststroke for
25 yards, butterfly for 25 yards and tread water for 5 minutes.
